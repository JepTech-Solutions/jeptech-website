Glossary

Every part and term the course uses, in plain words. Lessons and reference sheets use these words and no others.

Reference sheet · John's bicycle maintenance course · written 17 October 2026 · numbers link to the sources in the derailleur guide · see the parts map

Gears and drivetrain

Derailleur
The mechanism that moves the chain from one gear to the next. The rear one sits under the cassette; the front one sits above the chainrings. Sheldon Brown spells it "derailer".11
Cassette, cog
The cassette is the stack of toothed wheels on the back wheel; each wheel is a cog. "Smallest cog" is the fastest gear, "largest cog" the easiest climbing gear.1
Chainring (ring)
A toothed wheel at the pedals. The Defy has two (small ring, big ring); Sophia's bike and the Boardman have one.
1x ("one-by")
A bike with one chainring and no front derailleur, such as the Boardman.21
Speeds
The number of cogs on the cassette: 7 on Sophia's bike, 10 on the 105 5700, 11 on the Boardman. Shifter, derailleur, chain and cassette must match.19
Cross-chaining
Riding the small ring with the largest cog, or the big ring with the smallest cog. Avoided when riding, but used on purpose to set the front limit screws.13
Chain wear ("stretch")
The chain lengthens as its pins and rollers wear. Measured with a chain checker as a percentage; a worn chain shifts badly whatever you adjust.9
Pin, rivet
The short steel rods joining the links of a chain. Park Tool counts chain length in rivets: two rivets make one inch.8
Reinforced connecting pin
Shimano's special pin for joining a 10-speed chain such as the 105 CN-5701. Used once; never push an old one back in.12
Quick link, PowerLock
A two-part link that joins a chain without a tool. SRAM's PowerLock is for one use only.21

Parts of a rear derailleur

Hanger
The small metal tab the rear derailleur bolts to. On the Defy it is a separate bolt-on part (Giant 271200) that bends before the frame does.4,26
Dropout
The slot in the frame where the wheel axle sits.
Claw
A bracket for frames with no hanger: it hooks round the dropout and is held by the wheel's axle nut.2
Direct attach
A derailleur made to bolt straight onto a hanger, with no claw. The Altus RD-M310 is one.18
Mounting bolt
The bolt (5 mm hex key) that fixes the derailleur to the hanger.12
Cage
The pair of plates holding the two pulleys. A "long cage" wraps more chain, for wide-range cassettes such as the Boardman's 10–42.23
Guide pulley (upper pulley)
The upper of the two small toothed wheels in the cage, nearest the cogs. Every rear setting is judged by where it sits.1
Tension pulley (lower pulley)
The lower wheel in the cage; its spring keeps the chain taut.
Limit screws (H and L)
Two screws that stop the derailleur going too far. H (high) stops it at the fast gear, L (low) at the climbing gear. Tightening a limit screw closes its wall in.1,11
B-screw (B-tension screw)
A screw that rests on the hanger and tilts the derailleur, setting the gap between the guide pulley and the cogs. Tightening it makes the gap bigger.1
B-gap, chain gap
That gap, set on the largest cog. SRAM sets it with a plastic gauge.21
Wall
The course's word for the stop a limit screw sets: the furthest the derailleur can travel that way. "Small wall" is the small-gear stop, "big wall" the big-gear stop. Teacher's word, not a trade term.
B-adjust washer
On SRAM rear derailleurs, the washer behind the mounting bolt that the B-screw works against. It must sit flush on the hanger with no gap.21
Pulley bolts
The two small bolts that hold the pulleys in the cage; 2.5–5 N·m on the RD-5700.12
Cage Lock
A button on SRAM rear derailleurs that holds the cage forward so the chain goes slack, for removing the wheel or chain.21

Front derailleur

Cage plates (inner, outer)
The two side plates the chain runs between. The outer plate pushes the chain onto the big ring; the inner plate pushes it back to the small ring.3
Seat tube
The frame tube running down from the saddle to the cranks; the front derailleur sits on it.
Braze-on (-F)
A front derailleur that bolts to a tab on the frame. Shimano marks it with -F, as in FD-5700-F.15
Band (-B)
A front derailleur with its own round clamp for a plain seat tube, in 28.6, 31.8 or 34.9 mm. Shimano marks it with -B.15
Trim
One light click on the front shifter that moves the cage a little inward so the chain stops rubbing, without changing ring. On the ST-5700, a light press of lever b.13,14

Cables and shifters

Inner cable
The steel wire that the shifter pulls.
Housing (outer casing)
The tube the inner cable runs through. SP41 is Shimano's gear housing, required for the 105 5700 group.5,14
Ferrule
The metal or plastic cap on each end of a piece of housing.5
End cap
A small metal tube crimped on the cut end of the inner cable to stop it fraying.6
Cable fixing bolt, cable anchor
The bolt on the derailleur that clamps the inner cable. Shimano says fixing bolt, SRAM says anchor bolt.12,21
Barrel adjuster
A knurled (finely ridged, for grip) tube where the housing ends. Unscrewing it tightens the cable; screwing it in slackens it. There may be one on the derailleur, the shifter or in the housing.1,11
Inline adjuster
A barrel adjuster fitted in the housing, used where neither the lever nor the frame has one, as for the 105 front cable (Shimano SM-CA70 or SM-CA50).14
Indexing, SIS
Setting cable tension so each click moves the chain exactly one cog. SIS (Shimano Index System) is Shimano's name for it.12,17
STI, lever B and lever b
STI is Shimano's brake-and-gear lever for drop bars (the 105 ST-5700). Its small inner lever is called lever B on the right (rear) and lever b on the left (front); it releases cable. The main brake lever, pushed sideways inward rather than pulled, is lever A (rear: to a larger cog) and lever a (front: to the big ring).14,30
Free play
The first part of a shift lever's movement, before it starts pulling cable. Shimano's indexing check holds the lever at the end of its free play.12
Cable stop
A socket on the frame where a piece of housing ends and the bare inner cable carries on.
Lever clamp
The band that holds an STI lever to the handlebar; 6–8 N·m on the ST-5700.14
Rapidfire Plus
Shimano's flat-bar trigger shifter with two levers; it has its own barrel adjuster.20
Revoshift
Shimano's twist-grip shifter; no barrel adjuster on the shifter.20
Exact Actuation, X-Actuation
Two SRAM cable-pull ratios. Rival 1 needs an Exact Actuation shifter; SRAM's mountain-bike X-Actuation triggers do not match it.23,25

Tools and measures

Hex key (Allen key)
An L-shaped six-sided key. A 5 mm key is also 5 mm across its flats, so it doubles as a gap gauge.1
Torque, N·m
How hard a bolt is tightened, in newton metres. Matters most on the carbon Defy.
Torque wrench
A wrench you set to a torque; it clicks or slips when the bolt reaches it. Where the course gives a range, set the middle.
Chain checker
A gauge that drops into the chain and shows its wear as a percentage.9
Quarter turn
90°: the hex key moves from 12 o'clock to 3 o'clock. The course's standard step for limit screws.
Hanger alignment gauge
A long tool that screws into the hanger to check and straighten it, such as the Park Tool DAG. A bent hanger "can't be eyeballed", says SRAM.4,22
Tap
A hardened cutting tool that re-cuts a damaged thread, such as the hanger's; a shop job.2
Threadlocker
A liquid put on a bolt thread so it does not shake loose. Park Tool suggests a little on a Shimano derailleur bolt; SRAM says no grease on Rival 1's bolt.2,21
