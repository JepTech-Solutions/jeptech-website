Derailleur cheat sheet

Park Tool's techniques (Calvin Jones's videos) in Shimano's and SRAM's order, with their figures where they differ. Cogs and chain drawn to scale, derailleurs traced from photos, seen from where your eye goes. 18 October 2026.

Before any screw: hanger straight · chain not worn · cable runs freely · parts match. Adjusting cannot fix any of these.1,9

Rear

H · wire · L · B · index – Shimano's and SRAM's order: the gears are set last, so nothing after them moves them.5,7 Turn the cranks by hand; when indexing, at a normal pedalling pace.1

1 H limit big ring · smallest cog

From behind · shifter clicked fully out, to the smallest cog · B, H and L ringed5

Barrel adjuster on the derailleur in 2 turns: the cable goes slack, so only H decides where the derailleur stops.1

H in half a turn at a time until the chain rattles against the second cog, or hops onto it. Then out a quarter turn at a time until it runs quiet. If two settings are equally quiet, take the tighter.1

Check by eye: the guide pulley's centre is under the smallest cog's outer face.5,7

2 Wire

Shifter still clicked fully out · chain on the smallest cog

Screw the barrel back out the same 2 turns. Loosen the cable bolt on the derailleur, pull the cable taut by hand – firm, not hard – and re-tighten it (torque in the table).1,5

Click onto a few bigger cogs and back to bed the cable in – not onto the largest: L is not set yet. If the cable has gone slack, pull it taut again and re-tighten.

3 L limit small ring · largest cog

From behind · if the chain will not reach the largest cog, push the derailleur in by hand

Turn L, the lower ringed screw in step 1's picture, until the guide pulley's centre is under the largest cog's centre. L in moves the pulley out, away from the spokes.5,7

Then push the lever hard past its last click: the cage must not move further in. Too loose puts the chain in the spokes. Once indexed, a slow or noisy shift onto it: L out a quarter turn.1

4 B gap small ring · largest cog

From the drive side · cranks turned backwards, then still5

From the back of the bike, slide a 5 mm hex key's short end in beside the chain, between the guide pulley's top tooth and the cog tooth above it. It should just touch both: 5–6 mm.1,3 Take it out before the cranks turn.

B: the screw under the mounting bolt, ringed in step 1's picture.5 B in = bigger gap.1

Shimano: closest without touching, on the smallest cog too.5 Park: if it shifts well, it is fine.1

5 Index big ring · start on the smallest cog

One click, exactly one cog. No move: barrel out a turn. Two cogs: barrel in a turn.1 Unscrew to climb: barrel out moves the chain to bigger cogs.

Fine-tune on the 2nd cog: barrel out until the chain rattles on the 3rd, then in by quarter turns until quiet. Then every cog both ways: a rattle, barrel in a quarter turn.1

Shimano's check: on the 2nd cog, take up the lever's free play, short of the click: the chain should just rattle on the 3rd; let go and it is quiet.5

6 Ride

Every gear, both ways, pedalling hard. A workstand does not load the drivetrain like the road.1

SRAM Rival 1 (Boardman)

Same order. B: chain on the largest cog, SRAM's chain-gap gauge on the upper pulley, B-Adjust turned until the cog's tallest teeth line up with the gauge's groove; no millimetre figure.7 H, L and B-Adjust take a 3 mm hex.7 H and L sit side by side on the back, below the mounting bolt, H nearer the wheel; B-Adjust sticks out backwards at the top.7 Barrel: slow onto a bigger cog, out; slow onto a smaller cog, in.7

Front – 105 FD-5700-F on the Defy

Height and angle · L · wire · H · trim. Rear done first. On top of the body, L is the screw nearer the seat tube, H the one further out.6 Lever b is the small lever behind the left brake lever.6

1 Height

From the drive side · cage pulled out over the big ring by hand

Outer plate's lower edge about 2 mm above the big ring's tallest tooth; a hex key makes a gauge.2 To move it, loosen the mounting bolt.2,6

Park and Shimano: 1–3 mm.2,4,6 Lower shifts better, if it clears.8

2 Angle

From above · chain on the big ring

Outer plate parallel with the big ring. A long hex key held flat on the plate's outside shows the line.2,6

Back end of the cage too far in: turn the body anticlockwise seen from above; too far out: clockwise.2 Re-check height, then 5 N·m, Shimano's lowest; on carbon Shimano says ask the frame maker.6

3 L limit small ring · largest cog

From above · cable slack: barrel in 1–2 turns2

L in closes the gap: in until the chain rubs the inner plate, then out in small steps to no rub – about 1 mm.2,4

Shimano allows 0–0.5 mm.6 Once wired (step 4), shift up a ring and back: a slow drop means L out a quarter turn.2

4 Wire

Lever b pressed until it clicks no more · chain on the small ring

Loosen the cable bolt. Barrel adjuster screwed fully in, then out one turn. Pull the cable taut by hand and clamp it at 6–7 N·m. Shift a few times; if it has gone slack, re-pull and re-clamp.6

The Defy's front cable needs a barrel adjuster: at the down-tube stop, or an inline SM-CA70 or SM-CA50.6

5 H limit big ring · smallest cog

From above · lever a (up to the big ring) held pushed in

Start with H too loose; in a quarter turn at a time until the outer plate is about 1 mm from the chain at its closest as the cranks turn.2

Shimano: 0–0.5 mm.6 Slow shift up: H out a quarter turn.2 Rub only under hard pedalling is frame flex: leave it.4

6 Trim, then ride big ring · largest cog

Press lever b gently, only to its first light click (trim). Then the barrel: plate rubs the chain, barrel in; gap over 0.5 mm, barrel out. Aim for 0–0.5 mm.2,6

If trim drops the chain to the small ring: H out an eighth of a turn and repeat.6 Then shift both rings on several cogs, easing off the pedals.
